U.S. Debt Response

Switch between the two charts:

Data: FRED. Made in Python with Claude. The response of the US primary balance to lagged debt (γ), estimated separately for FY1950–1974, 1975–2000 and 2001–2025 in a single regression, controlling for the output gap and COVID. Following Bohn (1998), γ > 0 means primary surpluses rise with debt, a sufficient condition for sustainability. Bars are 95% confidence intervals.

Full specification

A single OLS regression over FY1950–2025: PBt = αk + β·Gapt + Σ γk·(Dt−1 × Ek,t) + δ·Covidt + εt, where Ek equals 1 in era k (FY1950–1974, 1975–2000, 2001–2025) and 0 otherwise.

PB is the primary balance and D is debt held by the public, both in % of GDP. Gap is the output gap against CBO potential GDP, and Covid is a FY2020–21 dummy. Each era has its own intercept αk, so γk measures the debt response within that era, while β and δ are shared across eras. Intervals use Newey-West (HAC, 2 lags) standard errors.

Data: FRED. Made in Python with Claude. The same debt response (γ), re-estimated over each 30-year window and plotted at the window's final fiscal year, controlling for the output gap and COVID. Below zero, primary surpluses no longer rise with debt. Shading is the 95% confidence interval; the lower panel shows the annual output gap. Latest window, FY1996–2025: γ = −0.067 (p = 0.004).

Full specification

Each point is an OLS estimate of PBt = α + β·Gapt + γ·Dt−1 + δ·Covidt + εt over the 30 fiscal years ending in that year, using data from FY1950–2025.

PB is the primary balance (federal surplus plus interest outlays, % of GDP), D is debt held by the public (% of GDP), Gap = 100 × (real GDP − CBO potential GDP) / potential GDP, and Covid is a FY2020–21 dummy. Following Bohn (1998), γ > 0 means primary surpluses rise with debt, a sufficient condition for sustainability. Intervals use Newey-West (HAC, 2 lags) standard errors.
